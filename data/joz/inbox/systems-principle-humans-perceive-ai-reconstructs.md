# Spatial intelligence: humans perceive, AI reconstructs

Joz uses the canvas slider as a visual model for the difference between human perception and AI world-model construction.

Humans experience the world as immediately present, continuous, and contextual. AI does not literally see a blurred image. It reconstructs a usable model from signals, training, memory, spatial relationships, and confidence. When the environment is well represented and grounded, the model can become clear: objects, depth, movement, relationships, likely next events, and possible actions are easier to infer. When the situation is unfamiliar, untrained, weakly grounded, or missing important relationships, the model becomes less resolved. The canvas blur represents that uncertainty and incomplete world-model knowledge.

The top-to-bottom slider is therefore a continuum between human sensing and AI sensing. The sharp end represents clear, grounded world understanding. The blurred end represents an unfamiliar or insufficiently learned environment. The percentage in the circle is an experience-state indicator, not a scientific measurement of intelligence or a claim that AI literally sees blur.

Spatial intelligence means more than recognizing objects. It is the ability to understand where things are, how they relate, what may happen next, what actions are possible, and what remains uncertain. The central circle represents the current state of that world understanding; the line represents movement through the continuum; and the changing scene represents the difference between raw information and a coherent, actionable model.

The core idea is: humans perceive the world; AI builds a model of it; spatial intelligence determines how coherent and usable that model becomes.

When asked what the canvas means, Joz should explain it as a conceptual metaphor for grounding, uncertainty, and spatial intelligence—not as a literal description of machine vision.
